Abstract

We built a decline-triage ranking system to help content editors prioritize which web pages to review first. Using 78.8 M rows of pseudonymized GSC and GA4 data (81,521 pages scored across 37 eligible clients), we constructed a feature table of five pre-decision signals (log impressions, log clicks, average position, active-day count, content age) and defined a proxy label: a page is declining if March impressions fell more than 20% below the prior 30-day window.

Across five client-grouped held-out folds, a Random Forest reached mean P@50 of 0.67 versus the frozen rule's 0.38 and the base rate of 0.25 — a clear same-window win. A time-aware walk-forward protocol (train Dec–Jan, test March) collapsed that to a mean fold of 0.26 — indistinguishable from random, with 4 of 5 folds below their own base rate. The ranking overfits to local volatility, not durable decay patterns.

The shipped product is the transparent rule — stale but visible pages ranked by traffic at stake — paired with a five-archetype human-review playbook and three monitoring triggers. Decision-support, not automation. Predictive, not causal.

01 Section

Research Question

Which web pages in a content portfolio are experiencing a structural decline in search traffic — and which should a human editor open first, within a fixed review budget?

Content teams operate under capacity constraints. A reviewer can open ~20–100 pages per cycle, not thousands. The question is therefore a ranking problem: given today's data, produce a prioritized queue where declining pages are concentrated at the top.

The honest metric is Precision@K: of the top-K pages the system recommends, how many truly declined? This is fixed before training. K ∈ {20, 50, 100} matches realistic review budgets.

The decision this supports

Actor: A content editor or SEO manager with a weekly review budget of ~20–100 pages.

Action: Open the page, read the reason code and risk flags, decide whether to refresh, rewrite the snippet, or monitor.

What ML provides: A ranked queue. Not a diagnosis. Not a guarantee. Not a causal claim.

Three real numbers that motivate the problem

  • 54.2% of pages in the starter dataset show a downward trend — not a rare-event problem. Enough signal to learn from.
  • 3× gap in the starter pipeline: a transparent hand-rule achieves P@50 of 0.24; the Random Forest reaches 0.74 on the same held-out clients. That gap is worth investigating.
  • 731 median impressions per page across 32 clients — most pages have real search exposure worth protecting. 1,205 rows carry no position data (avg_position == 0), handled as a missing-data flag, not rank zero.
Careful words — what this work cannot claim

Refreshing a recommended page does not cause recovery — no experiment, no causal design. The model does not predict Google's algorithm. The label is a proxy computed from the current window, not a true forward outcome. Early exploration (w01–w02) used the 30k-row starter slice; all deployed numbers here come from the full 78.8M-row warehouse.

03 Section

Methodology

Label definition

is_declining = 1 if March impressions fell more than 20% below the prior 30-day window: imp_last30 < 0.80 × imp_prev30. This is a proxy — computable at decision time, correlates with pages requiring intervention, and does not leak future information when features are constructed from the closed Jan 30 – Feb 28 window.

Whole-frame decline rate: ~25%. This is the floor every ranked system must beat.

Features (5, all pre-decision)

Feature Meaning Why it's safe
log_imp_prev30 Log-scaled impressions in the feature window Computed from Jan 30 – Feb 28 only; denominator of the label — noted as label-cousin
log_clk_prev30 Log-scaled clicks in the feature window Same window; click/impression ratio tested for leakage in w06
pos_prev30 Average GSC position in the feature window; 0 = no data Pre-decision; missing filled as 0 (documented)
days_with_imp_prev30 Active days (≥1 impression) in 30-day window — consistency signal Pre-decision; no future information
content_age_days Days since publication as of March 1 2026 Computed from content_created_date, knowable at decision moment

Models compared

  • Frozen rule (baseline): stale_but_visible — age ≥ 90d AND impressions ≥ 500 → score = imp_prev30. Human-readable. The bar to beat.
  • Logistic Regression: Readable weights, one per feature. Failed to beat the rule (mean P@50 ≈ 0.15).
  • Depth-3 Decision Tree: Readable logic. Marginal improvement over the rule (mean P@50 ≈ 0.42).
  • Random Forest (300 trees, min_samples_leaf=5): Best same-window P@50 ≈ 0.67. Collapses under time-aware validation.

Validation protocols

Protocol 1 — Client-grouped cross-validation (same window)

Five deterministic hash folds: every client sits on exactly one side. Train on four folds, evaluate on the fifth. Same K, same tie policy, same test rows as the frozen baseline. This is the fairest same-window test — but it still trains and tests in the same calendar period.

Protocol 2 — Time-aware walk-forward (the audit that matters)

Train on Dec–Jan features (label = Jan–Feb impressions); test on the March frame. This is the realistic deployment scenario: the model is asked to rank pages it has never seen, in a month it has never been trained on. This is where performance collapsed.

04 Section

Results

The same-window win is real. It does not survive a forward leap in time.

Protocol Base rate Rule P@50 Forest P@50
Random selection (base rate) 0.25 — —
Same-window, held-out clients (mean of 5 folds) 0.29 0.38 0.67
Time-aware, held-out clients — mean fold 0.29 — 0.26
The key finding

The Random Forest ranked 4 of 5 held-out-client folds above the rule in the same window. Under the time-aware protocol the mean fold fell to 0.26 — indistinguishable from random, with 4 of 5 folds below their own base rate. Shipped blind across all clients next month, the ranking would offer no advantage over random selection. Human verification is not optional garnish; it is what makes the queue useful.

Why the model collapsed

  • Search traffic is mean-reverting: pages that dipped in Feb often bounced in March regardless of their features.
  • Correlation sign flip: the relationship between log_clk_prev30 and the label flips sign between the Dec–Jan training window and the March test window — confirmed in code.
  • Fold variance is huge (time-aware fold P@50 ranged 0.04 → 0.56; same-window forest folds ranged 0.26 → 1.00): some clients' queues are near-useless; the average hides the spread.
  • Label-cousin proximity: log_imp_prev30 is the label's own denominator. A cousin-free forest (position, age, active days only) shrank the lead but did not reverse it — the signal is real but partly structural.
05 Section

Limitations

  • C1 — Time-aware collapse The model's forward validity is not established. Same-window P@50 0.67 collapses to 0.26 mean fold under a time-aware split — no better than random. The ranking is decision-support for the current period, not a forward predictor.
  • C2 — No causality This is observational data. Association between age/impressions and the decline label does not imply that refreshing a page will reverse the decline. No experiment, no causal design.
  • C3 — Proxy label is_declining is a 30-day impression ratio. Pages near the 80% threshold flip label over noise; seasonal dips look like decay. The label is a triage signal, not ground truth about content quality.
  • C4 — One portfolio, pseudonymized Findings are observed in this dataset only. Hash IDs carry no meaning — a human must open the actual page before acting.
  • C5 — Window instability Feature–label correlations change direction between calendar windows. The queue's signal is local to the training period.
  • C6 — Sealed final month The last month of the warehouse stays out of any development loop. It is an untouched holdout.
06 Section

Action Playbook

Because the model does not transfer forward, the deployed product is the transparent rule — with a structured human layer on top.

Five archetypes → one action each

Priority is first-match-wins down the table. Every page gets exactly one archetype and one action. Row-level reason codes (stale_but_visible, not_stale, low_volume) record how the transparent rule scored each page. Counts come from the w07 receipt.

No-go list

# Never automate Why
N1 Publishing or rewriting content from scores Time-aware mean fold P@50 0.26 ≈ random; errors would ship at scale
N2 Client-facing claims ("we predict declines") This ranks pages for review; it predicts nothing causally
N3 Acting on any bucket with n < 30 Small-n results are unreliable
N4 Scoring thin-history pages into actions Enforced structurally by archetype #1; re-verified in code
N5 Tuning against March or the final month Sealed test windows — tuning manufactures accuracy
N6 Bulk execution without per-batch sign-off One named reviewer owns each batch and its verdict log
N7 Putting this notebook on a scheduler Research decision-support; productionizing needs the retrain gate first
07 Section

Monitoring & Retrain Policy

The queue expires on a clock. Every trigger is checked before a fresh queue ships — never after.

# Trigger Metric Threshold Action
M1 Queue age Days since feature snapshot > 45 days Re-pull features, rescore, re-audit before shipping
M2 Ranking health Precision over human verdicts, rolling 3 batches < 0.25 base rate Halt queues; investigate
M3 Window instability Time+grouped mean fold P@50 < 0.30 Retrain + full audit — already fired on W6 window
M3 has already fired

The time-aware walk-forward audit (w06) produced a minimum fold P@50 of 0.04 and a mean of 0.26, triggering M3. This is why the playbook ships decision-support only, with per-batch human sign-off, and why any future model update must clear a fresh time+grouped audit before any queue ships.

Retrain policy

  • Retrain only when M3 fires, OR when ≥ 2 new labeled months exist.
  • Train on the newest closed months, excluding the sealed final month.
  • A time+grouped audit must show mean fold P@50 ≥ 0.30 before any queue ships.
  • A named human reviewer signs off before each batch.
